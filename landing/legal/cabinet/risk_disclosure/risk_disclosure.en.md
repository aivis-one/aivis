# Risk disclosure for buyers and integrators

## Who gives this notice and to whom {#rd-h-intro}

{company.name}, identification number {company.id}, legal address: {company.address} (the Company), e-mail {contact.general}, informs a buyer, a prospective buyer or an integrator in the Cabinet (together, the Reader) of the risks connected with buying a {sankord.name} licence and with working as an integrator. {sankord.name} is a software product of the Company, sold as a licence.

A licence is not a share, a participation or an investment, and it brings no return. The Cabinet sells no investments. A person who has signed an investment agreement with the Company outside the Cabinet is told the risks of that agreement in that agreement’s own risk notice.

The Reader should commit only an amount the loss of which he can afford, and should weigh a purchase against his other needs. Please take the following risks into account.

## 1. The product is new {#rd-h-new}

{sankord.name} has not yet been put into service at any customer. The architecture works every day in the founder’s own build, today on another company’s platform; the Company’s own runtime is still being assembled. The statements about what the system will do describe its design, and some of them have not yet been shown at a customer.

## 2. Dates are a plan {#rd-h-dates}

{sankord.plan} The Reader should not rely on a date. The demo version or the start of sales may come later than planned, or not as planned.

## 3. The Reader’s payments {#rd-h-pay}

The price and the terms of payment are those shown at the purchase and agreed in the order. A licence becomes perpetual when it is paid in full; until then it is not, and a missed payment switches nothing off. After the product has been installed for the Reader the price is not refunded, except where the law gives the Reader a right to it, and the fee for identity verification is never returned. The Reader may lose what he has paid if the product, the Company or the Reader’s own plans fail. A payment in digital assets, once confirmed on the network, may be impossible to recall.

## 4. Dependence on the founder {#rd-h-founder}

The Company’s activity and the development of {sankord.name} depend to a great extent on its founder and director, {company.director}. If he becomes unable to work for the Company, the Company and the product may be adversely affected.

## 5. A young company {#rd-h-company}

The Company was registered on {company.registered} and is small. It may fail, be unable to support the product, or change it, and an insolvency would put at risk what the Reader has paid and the support the Reader expects.

## 6. Software and artificial intelligence {#rd-h-ai}

{sankord.name} is made of AI agents. Agents can make mistakes, and the models they use can be wrong, change or become unavailable. The Reader remains responsible for the decisions he takes with the system and for checking its work. Models and services of third parties are used under their own terms, which the Company does not control.

## 7. Integrators {#rd-h-integ}

A buyer who uses an integrator should know that the integrator is an independent contractor, not the Company’s representative, and that the quality of his work and what he bills for it are not guaranteed by the Company.

A person who becomes an integrator should know that the share he receives depends on the payments that buyers actually make, that he stops receiving it for a buyer he stops supporting, that qualification can be lost, and that the Company does not promise him any income.

## 8. Regulatory and tax risk {#rd-h-reg}

New laws or regulations, or changes in existing ones, in Georgia or in the Reader’s country, governing software, artificial intelligence, data, payments or taxes, may have adverse consequences for the Company or for the Reader. Taxes and duties on a purchase or on an integrator’s income are the Reader’s own.

## 9. Currency risk {#rd-h-currency}

Amounts are stated in the currency shown at the purchase. A Reader who pays or counts in another currency bears the risk of an adverse change in exchange rates.

## 10. Sanctions and verification {#rd-h-sanctions}

The Company may refuse a purchase, hold or reverse a payment, or close an account where identity verification fails or the AML policy or a sanctions rule requires it. This can delay or prevent a purchase.

## 11. The Cabinet {#rd-h-cabinet}

The Cabinet may be unavailable at times, and data kept in it may be lost despite backups. Operational failures, errors of staff and failures of the Company’s partners may cause loss.

## 12. Information, not advice {#rd-h-info}

What the Company publishes about {sankord.name} is information for the Reader’s own decision and is not a promise of a result. It is current as of its date and may be updated without notice. Forward-looking statements, such as plans, dates and prospects, are not a guarantee of future results, which may differ materially. Nothing the Company publishes is a recommendation to establish a legal relationship with it; the Reader should rely on the agreements and documents he accepts in the Cabinet.

The Reader should make sure this notice is clear to him and, where necessary, obtain explanations from the Company or from an adviser. This notice may be supplemented by the Company; the text valid when the Reader accepts it is recorded in his account. The English text prevails over the text in any other language.
