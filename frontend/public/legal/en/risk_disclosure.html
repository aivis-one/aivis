1. The product is new

SANKORD has not yet been put into service at any customer. The architecture works every day in the founder’s own build, today on another company’s platform; the Company’s own runtime is still being assembled. The statements about what the system will do describe its design, and some of them have not yet been shown at a customer.

2. Dates are a plan

We plan the demo version for November 2026 and the working version, with licences on sale, from 1 January 2027. These dates are a plan, not a promise. The Reader should not rely on a date. The demo version or the start of sales may come later than planned, or not as planned.

3. The Reader’s payments

The price and the terms of payment are those shown at the purchase and agreed in the order. A licence becomes perpetual when it is paid in full; until then it is not, and a missed payment switches nothing off. After the product has been installed for the Reader the price is not refunded, except where the law gives the Reader a right to it, and the fee for identity verification is never returned. The Reader may lose what he has paid if the product, the Company or the Reader’s own plans fail. A payment in digital assets, once confirmed on the network, may be impossible to recall.

4. Dependence on the founder

The Company’s activity and the development of SANKORD depend to a great extent on its founder and director, Artem Baliakno. If he becomes unable to work for the Company, the Company and the product may be adversely affected.

5. A young company

The Company was registered on 12 February 2026 and is small. It may fail, be unable to support the product, or change it, and an insolvency would put at risk what the Reader has paid and the support the Reader expects.

6. Software and artificial intelligence

SANKORD is made of AI agents. Agents can make mistakes, and the models they use can be wrong, change or become unavailable. The Reader remains responsible for the decisions he takes with the system and for checking its work. Models and services of third parties are used under their own terms, which the Company does not control.

7. Integrators

A buyer who uses an integrator should know that the integrator is an independent contractor, not the Company’s representative, and that the quality of his work and what he bills for it are not guaranteed by the Company.

A person who becomes an integrator should know that the share he receives depends on the payments that buyers actually make, that he stops receiving it for a buyer he stops supporting, that qualification can be lost, and that the Company does not promise him any income.

New laws or regulations, or changes in existing ones, in Georgia or in the Reader’s country, governing software, artificial intelligence, data, payments or taxes, may have adverse consequences for the Company or for the Reader. Taxes and duties on a purchase or on an integrator’s income are the Reader’s own.

9. Currency risk

Amounts are stated in the currency shown at the purchase. A Reader who pays or counts in another currency bears the risk of an adverse change in exchange rates.
